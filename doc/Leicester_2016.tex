\begin{abstract}
\noindent We label every frame of BAR--ATH as \textbf{H}/\textbf{A}/\textbf{D} (\textbf{X} outside
the halves) with gradient-boosted classifiers trained \emph{only on other games}, and decode whole
possessions with a three-state Viterbi. The key step is making clean, labelled tracking from other
games \emph{look like} the competition's automatic tracking (ATD): the ball is hidden mostly while
play is dead and is low-pass filtered, exactly as measured on the unlabelled ATD. Team-shape and
ball-touch features, a 5-model ensemble and possession-level decoding give
\textbf{\spf{} 0.682 / \mf{} 0.932} in leave-one-game-out validation on the Metrica sample games
(mean of three seeds). No human label of BAR--ATH, and none of its public ground truth, is used to
train, select or tune anything.
\end{abstract}


\section{Approach and why}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The ball is missing on 41\,\% of graded ATD frames}, in $\sim$150 long gaps (median
  10.7\,s). ``Nearest player to the ball'' says nothing there; the owner has to be inferred from
  team shape (who pushes up, who is compact, how fast everyone moves). That is pattern recognition,
  so we learn it from labelled games.
\item \textbf{No label of BAR--ATH may be used}, so labels must come from other games, whose tracking
  is far cleaner than the ATD. Making it look like the ATD (Sec.~\ref{sec:degrade}) mattered more
  than any feature or model change.
\item \textbf{\spf{} rewards whole possessions, not frames.} Per-frame argmax flickers many times a
  minute (Fig.~\ref{fig:timeline}). A decoder with transition penalties is the simplest way to trade
  a little frame accuracy for clean possession boundaries.
\item \textbf{Features are team-level} (centroids, spreads, speeds, nearest-player distances, counts
  near the ball), so the ATD's 46 anonymous, fragmenting track slots do not matter.
\item \textbf{Ball-touch features} catch short possessions that team shape reacts to too slowly. A
  touch is a peak of the ball's velocity change ($>4$\,m/s over 0.24\,s) with a player within
  2.5\,m. From touches we derive the team of the previous/next touch, time since/until it, touch
  balance and counts per team in $\pm$1\,s and $\pm$3\,s, and which team is nearer the ball.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Labels from Metrica events.} The owner of the alive ball is the team of the most recent
on-ball event (pass, carry, shot, set piece, recovery, ball lost, ball out). The ball is dead from
the end of the event before a set piece until it is taken; for a foul from the offence. Outside
kick-off\,$\to$\,last event of each half the label is X.

\paragraph{Two definitions.} IDSSE marks more dead time (41--52\,\% vs 25--33\,\% of frames) and
more, shorter possessions. It still helps on the Metrica games (+0.010 \spf{}, all seeds), so it is
used for both models. Validation test games are always Metrica, the organisers' definition.

\section{Making training data look like the ATD}
\label{sec:degrade}

Every degradation parameter was set from \emph{unlabelled} ATD statistics only
(Table~\ref{tab:degrade}, Fig.~\ref{fig:smoothing}). The clean ball is first interpolated through
its own gaps: in the sample data it is missing almost exactly when dead, which would leak the label.
Two degraded copies are made of each training game. Gradient boosting handles the NaNs natively, and
features carry context across a gap (time since/until the ball was seen, ball position and
nearest-player gap carried forward and backward).

\centering\footnotesize

\midrule

  visible; a 2-component mixture gives $P(\mbox{miss}\mid\mbox{dead})\approx0.95$--$1$,
  $P(\mbox{miss}\mid\mbox{alive})\approx0.06$ &
  state-dependent mask: dead spells hidden ($\pm$1\,s jitter at the edges), alive play loses the
  ball 6\,\% of the time \\
smooth positions (median 2nd difference 0.011\,m ball, 0.001\,m players) &
  Gaussian noise smoothed over $\sim$1\,s, not white jitter \\

\bottomrule

\paragraph{Smart Tagging.} Set-piece tags give restart anchors and phase tags give a noisy ``team
believed in possession''. The training games have no Smart Tagging, so an equivalent noisy context
is synthesised from their events: 60\,\% of set pieces kept with $\pm$0.5\,s jitter, 80\,\% of
possessions tagged, 8\,\% with the wrong team. The model thus \emph{learns} how far to trust a tag.

\section{Model and decoding}

Two gradient-boosted tree models (scikit-learn \texttt{HistGradientBoosting}, 300 rounds, 31
leaves): a \emph{dead} model $P(D\mid\mbox{frame})$ and an \emph{owner} model
$P(\mbox{team }T\mbox{ has the ball}\mid\mbox{alive})$. The owner model is applied from both teams'
points of view and the two answers are combined, so it is symmetric by construction. Five such pairs
are trained on copies degraded with different seeds and their probabilities averaged.

A three-state Viterbi then maximises $\sum_t \log P(s_t\mid\mbox{frame}_t)$ minus transition costs:
20 nats for H$\leftrightarrow$A and 16 for entering or leaving D, separately in each half.
Fig.~\ref{fig:timeline} shows what this buys: the frame-level argmax is mostly right but splits
possessions into many fragments, each of which would be an unmatched spell.

\begin{figure*}[t]

  per-frame argmax and the decoded output. Bottom: model probabilities. The decoder removes the

\section{Validation and model selection}

Leave-one-game-out: train on two Metrica games (+ all IDSSE), test on the held-out Metrica game
degraded the same way, score with the official \texttt{possession\_metrics}. \textbf{All choices
were made here.} The public 5-minute window of BAR--ATH was only used to check that the pipeline
runs end to end, never to select or tune.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/ablation.pdf}
  \caption{Leave-one-game-out \spf{} as components are added. \mf{} of the final model: 0.932
  (0.929 / 0.933 / 0.935), higher than without the ensemble on every seed.}
  \label{fig:ablation}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation} summarises the steps. Rows that change the degradation (smoothing) also
change the degraded test games, so they are not strictly comparable with the rows above; they were
chosen because they match ATD statistics, not for their score. A random, state-independent ball mask
gives 0.579. Rows that change only data or features share the test set: IDSSE +0.010 and touch
features +0.016, positive on every seed; the ensemble +0.005 (+0.005 / +0.012 / $-$0.002), adopted
because it lowers variance and raises \mf{} on every seed. The decoder penalties are the centre of a
flat optimum of a $6\times5$ grid (all cells within 0.007).

\section{Possession rules implemented}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Loose balls, deflections, aerial duels:} stay with the team last in control until the
  other team makes a controlled on-ball action. The switch penalty stops a single touch by the
  defending team from creating a possession.
\item \textbf{Challenges / tackles:} possession changes only when the winning team keeps the ball. A
  foul makes the ball dead from the offence until the free kick is taken.
\item \textbf{Restarts:} dead from the stoppage until the set piece is played; the restart belongs
  to the team taking it (kick-off, free kick, corner, goal kick, throw-in).
\item \textbf{Outside the halves:} X, from the published half boundaries.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/recall.pdf}
  \caption{Share of true possessions recovered (IoU $\geq0.5$, same team) by length, three held-out
  Metrica games. Short possessions are the main \spf{} loss.}
  \label{fig:recall}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Too few possessions.} On validation we predict fewer possessions than there are
  (265 / 249 / 284 vs 379 / 357 / 404 per game). Long possessions are recovered almost always, short ones rarely
  (Fig.~\ref{fig:recall}): most missed short ones are given to the other team by the frame model,
  the rest are merged into a neighbour by the decoder.
\item \textbf{Turnovers inside a ball gap} are inferred from team shape alone and often placed late.
\item \textbf{Only 3 games share the target definition.} The degradation is matched to ATD
  statistics, not learned; IDSSE draws the dead-ball and possession lines differently.
\item \textbf{With more time:} more games labelled in Metrica's definition, a turnover-specific
  detector for short possessions, and ball detection in the tactical video to shrink the gaps.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reproduction}

Full instructions are in \texttt{possession/README.md} (Python 3.14.10, pinned requirements):

{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
pip install -r requirements-possession.txt
# competition bundle -> data/ ; Metrica samples ->
#   reference/sample-data (commit e706dd5);
# IDSSE -> data/external/idsse (command in README)
python -m possession.run --out Leicester_2016.csv
python evaluation/validate_submission.py \
       Leicester_2016.csv
python -m possession.validate --idsse all --ens 5
\end{verbatim}}

All randomness is seeded (one stream per training game, fixed model seeds). Re-running gives a
byte-identical file, md5 \texttt{0b3ed1253f1aeb5424e10b226b7c5e99}, checked by regenerating twice.
